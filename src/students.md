# Why attend as a student?

Your course teaches you how to build or design. FFConf puts you in a room with the people who do it for a living and shows you what they really care about.

## What you get from the day

**Every talk, no clashes.** FFConf is a single track. You won't have to choose between sessions or worry you picked the wrong one.

**Beyond the syllabus.** Talks cover accessibility, ethics, privacy, AI, CSS, JavaScript, careers and wellbeing: the thinking that shapes how the web gets built, from people doing the work.

**People who've been where you are.** Our audience and speakers include career changers, bootcamp graduates and developers who first came on a scholarship.

**Meeting the industry without the "networking".** Breaks, lunch and the post-social are where conversations with working developers, designers, product people, and future colleagues, happen naturally.

## In their words

Nicola Adele Simmonds attended in 2023 while studying for a Masters in Web Design. She found the opening talk on imposter syndrome:

> made it feel like the conference was curated just for me

She left with practical changes to make to her own sites. [Read Nicola's write-up](https://web.archive.org/web/20240620013834/https://adelei.co.uk/journal/2023/11/15/a-student-goes-to-ffconf/).

Calie Rushton came in 2019 on a scholarship, her first conference and early in her career after a bootcamp course:

> each and every speaker inspired me, taught me something and made me think deeply

She found the single track led to more meaningful conversations. [Read Calie's write-up](https://dev.to/calier/what-happened-at-my-first-tech-conference-spoiler-alert-nothing-bad-94f).

Tereza K., a self-described shy introvert, first came on a scholarship in 2019 and returned to every FFConf since:

> it's rare that I feel relatively ok surrounded by strangers

[Read Tereza's write-up](https://dev.to/terezak/ffconf-2023-3jh0).

A past scholarship recipient told us:

> The inspirational speakers and welcoming community were a big part of giving me the confidence to keep coding, attend bootcamp and become a developer.

And from another attendee:

> I tell every junior engineer at work to go along, and every one of them comments on how much they loved it.
